Most "AI in marketing" advice stops at "use AI for content." That's not a strategy, it's a
tool choice. If you actually want to know whether AI is helping your funnel, you need
experiments with a hypothesis, a control, and a number you can point to afterward.

Here are three worth running this quarter.

## 1. AI-assisted subject line testing vs. your best copywriter

Most teams either fully automate subject lines or fully ignore AI for them. Instead, run a
four-way test: your copywriter's version, an AI-generated version, an AI-assisted version
(human edits an AI draft), and your current control. Four weeks, same list size, same send
time. The AI-assisted version usually wins — not because AI is better at voice, but because
editing is faster than drafting from scratch, which means more iterations per week.

**What to measure:** open rate, and more importantly, click-through rate on the actual offer.
Open-rate wins from AI subject lines are common and mostly meaningless if click-through
doesn't move.

## 2. Personalization depth vs. personalization trust

Add one layer of AI-driven personalization at a time — first name, then behavioral segment,
then predicted intent — and watch not just conversion but unsubscribe and spam-complaint
rate at each layer. Most teams stop measuring the moment conversion goes up. The layer where
unsubscribes start climbing faster than conversion is your actual ceiling, not a technical
limit.

**What to measure:** conversion lift *and* complaint rate, side by side, not on separate
dashboards nobody cross-references.

## 3. AI content velocity vs. brand voice decay

If you're using AI to scale content output, track a "voice consistency" score over time —
even a rough one, scored by a human reviewer against a rubric — alongside output volume.
Teams that scale AI content without this check almost always see volume climb for 8-10 weeks
before voice complaints start showing up in customer feedback or comments, by which point
the backlog of "off-brand" content is expensive to fix.

**What to measure:** output volume per week, plotted against a monthly voice-consistency
score. If volume keeps climbing while the score drops, you've found your real ceiling.

---

None of these experiments require new tooling — they require treating AI as a variable in an
experiment instead of a foregone conclusion. That's the actual gap between teams who get real
value from AI in marketing and teams who just add a line item to their software budget.
