Marketing teams still budget content by what it costs to make. That number is now close to zero, which means the budget conversation is measuring nothing. The real cost of content was never production. It's what publishing does to your audience's willingness to hear from you again.

Treat that willingness as an account. I call it **the attention ledger**: every piece you publish is either a deposit or a withdrawal, and the balance decides whether your next piece gets opened at all.

## Deposits and withdrawals

A deposit happens when the reader leaves with more than they came with. You taught them something they used. You showed them a number nobody else would publish. You said the true thing first, or said it plainer than anyone had. Deposits compound: they are the reason someone opens email number nine from you.

A withdrawal happens when you spend the reader's time and give nothing back. Restating the obvious. Publishing because the calendar said Tuesday. Fifty variations of the same listicle because the tool made it cheap. Each one feels free. None of them are. The reader keeps the ledger even when you don't: they stop clicking, then stop opening, then stop seeing you at all. Overdraft, in this account, is silence.

The cruel part: AI made withdrawals effortless. Any team can now produce a year of withdrawals in a week.

## The metric that follows

If production cost is dead as a metric, replace it with earn rate: of everything you published, what share earned a deposit? Not impressions. Not output volume. What share would a reader say made them smarter?

Two operational rules fall out of this model.

**Every brief states its deposit.** Before anything is made, the brief answers one question: what does the reader leave with that they didn't arrive with? If the brief can't answer it, the piece is a withdrawal wearing a content calendar as a disguise. Kill it there, where it's cheap.

**Kill rate is a health metric.** Teams that kill nothing publish everything, and teams that publish everything run an overdrawn ledger. When I ran content on a $1M+/month budget, the pieces we killed were as important to the results as the pieces we shipped. A rising kill rate usually precedes a rising conversion rate, because what survives is denser.

## Why this matters more every quarter

Attention was always the scarce side of the trade, but scarcity used to be masked by production cost: making things was hard, so nobody could flood the market. That governor is gone. Every inbox, feed, and search result is now flooded by teams mistaking free production for free distribution.

Which is exactly the opportunity. When everyone else is making withdrawals at machine speed, a brand that only publishes deposits stands out on arrival. The ledger is the moat, and it's one AI can't build for you, because the model can generate the words but it cannot decide whether they're worth someone's time.

That decision is the job. Everything else is production.
